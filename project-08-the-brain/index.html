Explore / 01 · Electrical & chemical signals

Meet the neuron.

Neurons are specialised cells that receive and transmit information. They communicate using electrical changes along the cell and chemical messengers across tiny gaps called synapses.

Signal simulator

Send a neural signal.

Adjust the firing rate, then trigger a pulse. The glowing signal travels down the axon. In a real neuron, an action potential is an all-or-nothing electrical event; firing rate and timing help encode information.

Explore / 02 · Specialised regions

A team of specialists.

Different brain areas are strongly associated with different functions. But they work in connected networks — no complex behaviour belongs to just one isolated spot.

Explore / 03 · Learning & memory

How does a memory form?

Memory is not a video recording. Learning changes connections across neural networks. The hippocampus is important for forming many new memories, while long-term storage is distributed across the brain.

What is happening?

Plasticity: the adaptable brain.

Neuroplasticity is the ability of the nervous system to change its structure and function with experience. It supports learning and adaptation throughout life.
