Project 04 / Engineering

F1 is a physics experiment at 300 km/h.

Every lap is a negotiation between airflow, grip, energy, temperature and human decision-making. Formula 1 turns those constraints into a racing machine.

Start exploringSee what changed in 2026 ↓
01 / Aerodynamics

Make air do the work.

A wing is not there simply to make the car fast. It shapes pressure and airflow to create downforce, letting the tyres push harder against the track.

DOWNFORCE

More grip

F1 wings generate aerodynamic load. More load can increase cornering force, but it also comes with a cost: drag.

Grip ↑ → corner speed ↑
Drag ↑ → straight-line speed ↓
DRAG

Air has a price

As the car pushes through the air, resistance grows. Engineers constantly search for the fastest compromise between low drag and useful downforce.

WAKE

What happens behind?

A fast car disturbs the air behind it. Managing that wake matters because turbulent air can make it harder for another car to follow closely.

02 / Energy

The car is an energy system.

Modern F1 performance is not just about burning fuel. Drivers and engineers manage electrical energy, harvesting it under braking and deciding when to deploy it.

03 / Tyres

Four contact patches.

Almost everything the car does ultimately has to pass through four tyre contact patches. Temperature, load and compound all change the available grip.

TEMPERATURE

Find the window

Tyres perform best within a useful operating range. Too cold and grip can suffer; too hot and performance can fall away.

LOAD

Push harder

Downforce increases the load on the tyres, allowing more force through the contact patch while cornering and braking.

TRADE-OFF

Speed vs life

A tyre that gives peak performance may not be the tyre that lasts the longest. Race strategy turns tyre behaviour into a timing problem.

04 / One lap

What is happening every second?

The car continuously changes state. Braking, turning, accelerating, harvesting and deploying energy are all connected.

01 / BRAKE

Slow down

Braking transfers load forward and can recover electrical energy through the hybrid system.

02 / TURN

Generate grip

Downforce and tyre load help the driver carry speed through the corner.

03 / EXIT

Deploy

Traction and electrical deployment work together to accelerate the car.

04 / STRAIGHT

Trim drag

In 2026, active aero can switch toward a lower-drag configuration on designated straights.

A new F1 philosophy.

2026 combines a major chassis reset with a major power-unit reset, changing the engineering problem teams are trying to solve.

ACTIVE AERO

Corner Mode vs Straight Mode

Movable front and rear wing elements can change configuration. The goal is downforce when cornering and lower drag when straight-line speed matters.

OVERTAKE MODE

Energy becomes strategy

When a driver is within one second at the detection point, Overtake Mode can provide extra electrical energy on the following lap. It replaces the role DRS played in aiding overtakes.

SUSTAINABILITY

Advanced sustainable fuel

The 2026 cars use advanced sustainable fuel while the power units move toward a much larger electrical contribution.

The Big Idea

F1 is not one technology.
It's the interaction.

Aerodynamics changes tyre load. Tyre load changes corner speed. Corner speed changes braking and acceleration. Braking changes recovered energy. Energy changes strategy. Strategy changes what the driver does next. The fastest car is the one that makes all of those systems work together.
