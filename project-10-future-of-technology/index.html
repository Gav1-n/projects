01 / The landscape

Six frontiers to watch.

These fields are already moving from research labs into the real world. Their timelines are uncertain, but the direction is fascinating.

✳

AI & robotics

AI systems are becoming more capable at reasoning, creating and coordinating. Robotics brings those capabilities into the physical world.

Intelligence in action
◈

Quantum computing

Quantum processors use quantum states to tackle certain specialised problems in ways classical computers cannot efficiently match.

A different kind of computing
⌁

Biotechnology

Gene editing, cell therapies and engineered biology could transform medicine, agriculture and the materials we make.

Programming living systems
☼

Clean energy

Cheaper renewables, better batteries, smarter grids and new nuclear designs are reshaping how energy is produced and stored.

Powering a new era
⌖

Next-gen interfaces

Spatial computing, voice, wearables and neural interfaces could make interacting with digital systems feel more natural.

Beyond the screen
▧

Advanced materials

Materials engineered at tiny scales may be lighter, stronger, self-healing or better at conducting heat and electricity.

Matter with new abilities

04 / Looking ahead

2035 → 2050.

Not a prediction calendar. A set of plausible directions, dependent on scientific progress, investment, policy and public choices.

2026+

Today’s foundations

AI assistants, early humanoid robots, grid-scale storage and precision medicine continue to develop.

2030s

Systems connect

More autonomous workflows, smarter infrastructure and tailored therapies may become part of everyday services.

2040s

New capabilities

Some advanced materials, interfaces and energy technologies could mature — if key engineering barriers are solved.

2050

Choices compound

The shape of the future will reflect not just invention, but who benefits, what is protected and how technology is governed.

05 / The human side

Progress raises questions.

Powerful technology is never just a technical story. The rules, incentives and people around it matter just as much.

◎

Who benefits?

Will new capabilities be widely accessible, or concentrated among a small number of organisations and countries?

⌑

What do we protect?

Privacy, security, human agency, biodiversity and the environment need to be designed into systems from the start.

✳

What stays human?

As machines take on more tasks, societies will need to decide what work, relationships and decisions should remain human-led.
